Complete Rules

Manage Score, Tempo, and Cards Across Three Rounds

The tutorial can be skipped at any time. Full rules and combo conditions remain below.
1

Opening and Mulligan

Each side draws a balanced 12-card opening with at least two possible two-card basic combos. Replace up to 4 before round one. Draw 2 cards before both rounds two and three, then replace up to 1.

2

Actions on Your Turn

Take one action: double-click a hand card to play it, use your one-time leader ability, or pass. A single click only raises or lowers the card. Any formal action ends your turn.

3

Three Card Rows

Space cards build the layout, Ornament cards represent craft and belief, and Inscription cards represent plaques, couplets, and ritual systems. Method cards use research, fieldwork, restoration, and digital interpretation to adjust the hand or board; they are discarded after use and do not occupy a row.

4

What Passing Means

Once you pass, you cannot act again that round. Your opponent may continue until passing. Preserving cards can matter more than a few extra points.

5

Round Resolution

After both sides pass, the higher total wins the round. A tied round awards no marker to either side and proceeds to the next round or final result.

6

Victory Condition

The match lasts up to three rounds. The first side to earn two round markers wins. After round three, compare round markers; equal markers produce a draw.

7

Direct Connections

Some cards gain points immediately when paired, such as Front Hall with Painted Bracket Sets, Huatai with Five-Element Stones, or Rear Hall with Ancestral Tablets.

8

Heritage Combos

The game contains 24 two-card starter combos and 12 three- to four-card advanced combos. Conditions are listed in this guide; the board does not preview a completable combo, and feedback appears only after completion.

What Should You Take Away?

The hall’s value lies not only in an old building, but in the way lineage development, Hakka settlement, ritual practice, spatial order, written teaching, and timber craftsmanship remain together in one place. The combo system reconnects these layers.

Sources and Design Notes

Heritage Content Uses Documented Sources; Card Values and Combo Names Are Game Adaptations

Heritage Records

The Bureau of Cultural Heritage record for the Hsieh Ancestral Hall in Neipu, the Taiwan Cultural Memory Bank, and the project’s plans, photographs, and inscription study.

Open the National Cultural Heritage Database Open the Taiwan Cultural Memory Bank

Project Material

“Heritage Promotion and Digital Technology: The Hsieh Ancestral Hall in Pingtung” (Department of Cultural Heritage Conservation, National Yunlin University of Science and Technology, 2026).

The game adopts the project’s leader, space, ornament, and inscription categories, together with its links among layout, decorative vocabulary, and ritual writing.

Card power, effects, combo names, and bonus points are game design. They do not represent official heritage rankings, formal architectural terminology, or proven historical causation. Interview-based and uncertain content is labelled in the learning text.

Rules Basis

The core rhythm draws on multi-round play, mulligans, passing, and row-based resource management. This game uses its own rules: up to three rounds, a balanced 12-card opening, four card types, heritage combos, and learning paths.

Illustrations and Background

The background uses the provided photograph of the hall. Card art consists of original color illustrations designed for recognition and learning.
