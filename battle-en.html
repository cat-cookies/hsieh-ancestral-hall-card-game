    Complete Rules

    Manage Score, Tempo, and Cards Across Three Rounds

    The tutorial may be skipped at any time. Full rules and combo conditions remain below.
    1

    Opening and Mulligan

    Each side draws 10 cards. Replace up to 3 before round one, then 1 after the draws for rounds two and three.

    2

    Actions on Your Turn

    Take one action: double-click a hand card to play it, use your one-time leader ability, or pass. A single click only raises or lowers the card. Any formal action ends your turn.

    3

    Three Card Rows

    Space cards build the layout. Ornament cards show craft and belief. Inscription cards show plaques, couplets, and ritual writing.

    4

    What Passing Means

    Once you pass, you cannot act again that round. Your opponent may continue until passing. Preserving cards can matter more than a few extra points.

    5

    Round Resolution

    After both sides pass, the higher total wins the round. A tie awards one marker to each side.

    6

    Victory Condition

    The match lasts up to three rounds. The first side to earn two round markers wins.

    7

    Direct Connections

    Some cards gain points immediately when paired with related cards.

    8

    Major Combos

    Major combos are not previewed on the board. An animation appears only after completion.

    What Should You Take Away?

    The hall’s value lies not only in an old building, but in the way lineage development, Hakka settlement, ritual practice, spatial order, written teaching, and timber craftsmanship remain together in one place.

    Sources and Design Notes

    Heritage Content Comes from Documented Sources; Point Values Are Game Adaptations

    Heritage Records

    The Bureau of Cultural Heritage record for the Hsieh Ancestral Hall in Neipu, the Taiwan Cultural Memory Bank, and the project’s plans, photographs, and inscription study.

    Open the National Cultural Heritage Database Open the Taiwan Cultural Memory Bank

    Project Material

    “Heritage Promotion and Digital Technology: The Hsieh Ancestral Hall in Pingtung” (Department of Cultural Heritage Conservation, National Yunlin University of Science and Technology, 2026).

    The game adopts the project’s leader, space, ornament, and inscription categories, together with its links among layout, decorative vocabulary, and ritual writing.

    Rules Basis

    The round and resource system draws on a Gwent-style best-of-three structure, ten-card hands, mulligans, passing, and rows, adapted for education.

    Illustrations and Background

    The background uses the provided photograph of the hall. Card art consists of original color illustrations designed for recognition and learning.
